## 1. What this is and what is asked of you

Form No. 26 is the tax audit report under section 63 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (rule 47 of the Income-tax Rules, 2026). It replaces Forms 3CA, 3CB and 3CD from tax year 2026-27. ICAI VERA is adding it to the Tax Audit module.

The Tax Audit lead has ruled that the tool is built exactly as the office's fillable Form 26 has it. Before building, a study compared that file, and the office's Form 26 / Form 3CD mapping, with the official texts. The study made recommendations. **None of its recommendations on the form is applied.** They are set out here for you, the domain team, to rule on. The tool will be revised on your answers. (The mapping is treated differently: see Part Two.)

What is asked:

- Go through the points in Parts One to Four. Each stands on its own; you need not have seen the study.
- Under each point there is a **Decision** line. Tick one: **Keep as built**, **Apply the recommendation**, or **Other** (and say what). Add a remark if you wish.
- In Part Two the decision is a column of the table: write **Confirm** or the entry you want.
- Send the marked copy to the Tax Audit lead. The project records fix no date for the answers; the Tax Audit lead will say when they are needed.

If time is short, Part One items 1.1 to 1.3 matter most: the pre-filled answers, the reasons for a negative answer, and the two tables that the Gazette has numbered crosswise.

Three things to keep in mind while reading:

- "Notified form" means Form No. 26 as printed in the Gazette. Nothing here proposes to change a word that the form prints.
- Several oddities are slips in the Gazette itself. They are not mistakes of the office's file. Each is marked "Gazette's own slip".
- The department's FAQ and guidance note explain the form. They do not override it.

## 2. How the tool behaves today

Form No. 26 in the tool is built to match the office's fillable Form 26. It has the same Parts, clauses, schedules and tables. It starts with the same preset answers, and it adds the same totals and calculations. A schedule opens on the same answer as in the office's file. The wording is the office file's wording, which is the notified wording almost throughout, including the Gazette's own slips; the few places where the office's file words something differently are listed in Part One. The mapping is the one exception, by the Tax Audit lead's decision of 21 November 2026: until the team rules, the tool shows the mapping rows as corrected from the official texts, not as written in the office's mapping. By the owner's ruling of 22 November 2026 its outputs carry no draft notice; nothing is claimed about acceptance by the e-filing portal.

So, under each point of Part One, "What the tool does now" describes what the office's fillable Form 26 does.

## PART ONE: points on the form

### 1.1 Answers that are already filled in when a new form is opened (R-04, D-05)

**What the tool does now.** A new form opens with answers already set, and "Clear all" sets them again:

- All 67 Yes/No answers are set: 63 to "No" and 4 to "Yes". The four are clause 14(d) (rule 46(8) complied with), 50(a), 50(b) and 52.
- Category of assessee is set to (ii).
- Clause 9 is set to the first ground ("total sales, turnover or gross receipts of business exceeding specified limits under section 63").
- Clause 15(a) is set to "Mercantile system". Clause 16(a)(i) and (ii) are both set to "Lower of Cost and Net Realisable Value".
- Residential status (Part C, item 5) is set to "Resident".
- In the two audit reports, "profit and loss account" and "profit" are already selected.

A preset answer is counted as filled, so nothing warns the user that it has not been looked at.

**What the notified form says.** It presets nothing. Every Yes/No is printed as an open "Yes/No" and every choice as an open list. The department's FAQ 17 says: "Each clause in Part B requires a mandatory Yes/No response to ensure completeness and uniformity." (Its "Part B" is the notified Part D, clauses 9 to 53.)

**Recommendation of the study, and why.** Every answer starts blank, and a blank answer counts as unfilled until the user chooses. A preset answer can go out over the auditor's signature without anyone having considered it. Two examples. A preset "Yes" at 14(d) states that the daily backup is kept on servers in India (rule 46(8): "shall be kept on a daily basis in servers physically located in India"). A preset "No" at clause 36 (depreciation) would be wrong for almost every assessee. The office's own mapping note says the same: "In an office template these should be left blank, so that an answer not overwritten does not go out as a certified statement."

### 1.2 Reasons for a negative or qualified answer (R-05, D-06)

**What the tool does now.** The three notes that ask for reasons are printed, but there is no box for a reason beside any Yes/No answer. The only places for free text are the paragraph 5 table of the report, paragraph 3(a) of Part B, and the "Remarks" columns of some schedules.

**What the notified form says.** Part A Note 2 (p. 1876), Part B Note 2 (p. 1877) and Part D Note 6 (p. 1885), in the same words each time: "Where any of the requirements in this Form is answered in the negative or with qualification, give reasons therefor."

**Recommendation of the study, and why.** Offer a "Reasons / remarks" box whenever an answer is "No". Make it compulsory only (a) at clause 14(d) when the answer is "No", and (b) wherever a qualification or observation is entered. An ordinary "No" to a "Whether ..." or "Any ..." question would need no reason.

The study is unsure here, and says so. **No official text defines which answers are "negative".** It may mean every "No" (some sixty reasons in an ordinary audit), or only a "No" to a requirement, such as 14(d). The FAQ and the guidance note do not mention these notes at all; they treat "No" as a complete answer that simply opens no schedule. The same note stood in Forms 3CA and 3CB. The office's mapping note takes the wider view: "Every negative answer also needs reasons (Note 2)." The study adds that this is a professional judgement and that guidance of the Institute, if there is any, should prevail. Other answers it names as possible candidates for a compulsory reason: 12(c)(i) when no copy is attached, Schedule 46 column (D), and Schedule 40 column (E).

### 1.4 Clause 14(d) and (e): backup of electronic books in India (R-14, D-08)

**What the tool does now.** 14(d) is preset "Yes" (item 1.1). 14(e), the address of the backup server, is always shown, whatever the answer to 14(d). It begins "If yes" with a capital letter.

**What the notified form says.** (p. 1879) "(d) Whether provisions of rule 46(8) have been complied with? Yes/No" and "(e) if yes, furnish address of location where backup server is located in India". Only "Yes/No" is printed; there is no "Not applicable".

Rule 46(8) covers books "maintained in electronic mode", for business and profession alike. Its sub-rule (1) is "Every person required to keep and maintain books of account and other documents under section 62(1)(b)". FAQ 18 reads it the same way.

**Recommendation of the study, and why.** Keep the answer as "Yes/No" with no third option. When the answer is "No", require the reason (for example, that the books are not kept in electronic mode, or the actual non-compliance). Show 14(e) only when 14(d) is "Yes". The reason line keeps a "No" from reading as a breach where the rule simply does not arise. The form does not settle what to answer where no books are kept in electronic mode.

### 1.5 Which Parts each category of assessee fills (R-01, D-01). Gazette's own slip.

**What the tool does now.** It prints the two category lines exactly as notified. It then behaves like this: category (i) shows Parts A, C and D; category (ii) shows Parts B, C and D. It adds its own sentence saying so, which is not printed in the output.

**What the notified form says.** The category lines (p. 1875) end "(in such cases, fill Parts A, B and C)" for category (i) and "(in such cases, fill Parts A, B and D)" for category (ii). But Parts A and B are the two audit reports, Part C is the particulars of the assessee and Part D is clauses 9 to 53. Rule 47(1) (p. 1570) puts the report in Part A for a person audited under another law and in Part B for others, and rule 47(2) says: "The particulars required to be furnished under section 63 shall also be in Part C and D of Form No. 26." So the bracketed words cannot be followed literally: nobody gives both reports, and category (i) cannot leave out Part D.

The slip has a plain explanation. In the public-consultation draft the Parts carried other letters (A particulars, B clauses, C and D the two reports), and the category line was right for those letters. The notified form moved the reports to the front as Parts A and B and left the line as it was. The department's FAQ and guidance note still use the draft's letters.

**Recommendation of the study, and why.** Category (i) fills Part A, Part C and Part D; category (ii) fills Part B, Part C and Part D; the category line is printed exactly as notified. This is what the tool already does. The team is asked to confirm it, and to say whether the tool should show the user a line of explanation.

### 1.14 "Delete whichever is not applicable" and the number of branches (D-16)

**What the notified form says.** The alternatives carry a star, for example "the audited *profit and loss account/income and expenditure account" (Part A 2(a), p. 1875), and Part A Note 4 reads "4. * Delete whichever is not applicable." ## What happens after the review

1. The Tax Audit lead collects the team's answers and revises the tool on them. Until then the tool stays as the office's fillable Form 26 has it.
2. Each answer is recorded in the project's decision register as a decision, with its date and the item number used here.
3. Before each release the chain of amendments to the Income-tax Rules, 2026 and the list of changed law in Part Three are checked again, because either may have moved. The e-filing portal is checked again for a utility and for the business-code list.
4. Every output of the tool remains a draft. Nothing here is a claim that the portal will accept it, and nothing here replaces the auditor's own judgement.
